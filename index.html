What the data says

Five findings

AAFES moved $75.42M through Dept 1097 over the last 52 weeks, down 12.9% year over year. Inside that contraction, the streaming picture splits cleanly in two.

1

Roku is taking share in players while the category shrinks.

The three-brand player category did $1.48M, down 2.8%. Roku grew +11.3% to $618,388 and lifted share from 36.4% to 41.7%. Amazon fell 8.3% and gave up 3.1 points.

2

Roku passed Amazon in monthly sales in July and August.

Roku outsold Amazon Fire TV in each of the last two months on file — $46,300 vs $45,912 in July, $53,997 vs $43,673 in August. That is the first sustained crossover in the two-year series.

3

Roku's ASP is rising faster than Amazon's.

Roku ASP moved from $37.36 to $40.69, up +8.9%, on 15,196 units. Amazon's ASP is effectively flat at $46.74. Roku is trading the mix up; Amazon is holding price and losing volume.

4

On the TV wall, Roku OS has all but disappeared.

Roku-powered TV sets did $67,853 on 313 units — 0.1% of a $69.84M TV business, down 80%. Amazon entered the same wall this year with Hisense Fire TV sets at $125,224 from a standing start.

5

The small-screen online opportunity is smaller than the buyer's framing suggests.

TVs 50″ and under are 14.0% online — the least digital part of the department — and the segment is down 22.0% year over year. Ecommerce runs 27.5% department-wide and only clears 35% in November and December.

Coverage caution. Operating system is only reliable where AAFES states it in the item description. Samsung and LG are safely inferred from brand. TCL, Hisense and Element ship on several platforms and their newer AAFES descriptions (for example “TCL 55″ S5 S-Class”) name no OS at all — those dollars sit in Other/Unknown and VIDAA/Other rather than being assigned a platform. The Roku TV and Fire TV figures are firm, because every one of those items names the platform. The Google TV figure is a floor, not a ceiling.

Roku's licensed-OS position at AAFES has gone from thin to negligible: $67,853 on 313 units, against $346,637 and 2,175 units a year ago. Westinghouse carried nearly all of it, and Westinghouse as a brand fell 72% to $107,086. The prior review in July put Roku OS at roughly 0.4% unit share; it is now 0.22%.

The contrast with Amazon is the point. Amazon went from no TV-set presence at all to $125,224 on 462 units in a single year by landing the Hisense QD6 Fire TV line — and is carrying 1,092 units of on-hand inventory behind it. That is what an OS licensing win looks like in this channel, and it happened while Roku's position eroded.

Two things follow. First, the 35% figure describes the holiday peak, not the year — it is a seasonal spike, and the annual mix has not moved in two years. Second, and more useful for assortment planning: the small-screen tier is the least online-driven part of the department at 14.0%, down from 15.5%, while sets over 50″ run 32.1%. Big screens go in the cart online; small screens get picked up in the store. A small-screen strategy built around exchangeonline.com is aiming at the thinnest slice of a shrinking tier.

Implications

Where we would put the effort

1

Defend and press the player win.

Roku is the only one of the three growing, it has crossed Amazon in the last two months, and it is doing it on rising ASP. The refreshed 4776xxx line is carrying it. The near-term ask of AAFES is straightforward: protect facings on the growing SKUs, and get the Streambar SE and Ultra 4850R into more of the 156 doors already carrying Roku.

2

Treat OCONUS as the core, not the outpost.

40% of Roku player dollars come from overseas and deployed locations, and on-hand inventory is concentrated in Qatar, Jordan, Kuwait, Germany, Okinawa and Korea. That is a defensible position against a competitor with a broader CONUS retail footprint, and it is where the product solves a real problem.

3

Do not build the TV OS case on the online channel.

The premise that small-screen sells online does not hold here: 14.0% online and falling. If Roku wants TV OS placement at AAFES, the argument has to be won on the store planogram, in the sizes and price points AAFES actually turns.

4

If TV OS is the goal, the Hisense route is the proven one.

Amazon put $125,224 on the wall in one year through a Hisense QD6 line, not through a value-brand licensee. Westinghouse — Roku's carrier at AAFES — fell 72% and is now 0.15% of TV dollars. A Roku OS re-entry needs a partner AAFES already buys in volume.

5

Size the ask honestly.

The ≤50″ tier is $11.77M and shrinking 22% a year, with Samsung and LG taking share within it. A credible Roku OS target is a point or two of that tier, not the 15% framing from the July draft. The bigger dollar prize is defending the $618K player business, which is growing.

Methodology

How these numbers were built

Source
AAFES Atlas Inventory Trend Report (ITBIS), Dept 1097 “TV Video”, all stores, all suppliers. Extract generated Dept 1097 TV - Sept 1, 2026.xlsx — 59,841 item × location rows × 106 fiscal weeks. Metrics: Units, Sales $, EoP Store On Hand Units, EoP Store Extended Retail $.
Periods
Rolling 52 weeks W/E 2025-09-06 to 2026-08-29, against W/E 2024-09-07 to 2025-08-30. On-hand is a point-in-time balance as of 2026-08-29.
On-hand treatment
Raw on-hand nets negative because the ECOMM DROP SHIP FULF pseudo-location carries a −6,251 unit balance for vendor drop-ship, where AAFES owns no inventory. Drop-ship and returns locations are excluded from all on-hand figures. Note the feed reports store on hand only, so distribution-centre and ecommerce inventory is not visible.
Brand and platform assignment
Assigned from the AAFES item description. Streaming players are separated from TV sets that merely run the platform. Screen size is parsed from the description and, where absent, from the model number (Samsung UN/QN/MRN, LG OLED/UA/UT, Sony XR/KD, Hisense and TCL series codes). 92.5% of department dollars resolve to a TV set with a parsed size; the remainder is mounts, cables, surge, projectors and streaming devices.
Region
CONUS, OCONUS and Exchange online derived from the AAFES district code — four-digit districts beginning 5, 6, 7 or 9 are OCONUS (Puerto Rico, Pacific, Europe/Middle East, Alaska); districts described as ECOM are online. Hawaii and Alaska are counted OCONUS. The JBMDL Dover / Pituffik rollup is counted CONUS despite containing a Greenland location.
Known limits
TV operating system is only stated for some items; TCL, Hisense and Element sets whose descriptions name no platform are reported as unknown rather than assigned one. Apple TV is not carried in Dept 1097 and is absent from all share calculations. Figures are AAFES only — NEX, MCX, CGX and VCS are not in this extract.
Verification
Department totals, unit totals, ecommerce dollars and all three brand totals were recomputed directly from the source workbook by an independent script and reconcile exactly.
